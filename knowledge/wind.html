The Complete Wind Guide

Taming the Invisible Enemy of Outdoor Cooking

Why Wind Matters

Wind is the sneaky saboteur of grilling. While you're watching the temperature and checking for rain, wind quietly wreaks havoc on your heat distribution, flame control, and cook times. It accounts for 25% of your Grillability Index for good reason.

Heat Robbery

Wind carries heat away from your grill and food. Even moderate wind can drop your effective cooking temperature by 50-100°F, leading to longer cook times and inconsistent results.

Flame Chaos

Gas grills in wind create dancing, unpredictable flames. Charcoal grills burn unevenly as wind stokes one side while starving the other of oxygen.

Safety Hazards

Strong wind can blow embers onto flammable surfaces, knock over lightweight grills, and spread flames in unexpected directions. This isn't paranoia; it's physics.

Wind Speed Thresholds

Not all wind is created equal. Here's how different wind speeds affect your grilling:

0-5 mph (0-8 km/h) Calm
Score: 100/100

The Sweet Spot: Barely perceptible wind. Smoke rises straight up. Flames behave predictably. Your napkins stay on the table. This is grilling nirvana.

5-10 mph (8-16 km/h) Light Breeze
Score: 75-100/100

Still Comfortable: You can feel it on your face. Leaves rustle. Minor adjustments might be needed, but nothing that should concern an average griller. Keep an eye on light items that might blow away.

10-18 mph (16-29 km/h) Moderate Wind
Score: 40-75/100

Pay Attention: Small branches move. Dust and loose paper become airborne. You'll need to position your grill strategically and expect some temperature fluctuation. Skilled grillers can handle this; beginners should be cautious.

18-25 mph (29-40 km/h) Strong Wind
Score: 15-40/100

Challenging: Large branches sway. You feel resistance when walking. Grilling is possible but frustrating. Heat control becomes a constant battle. Consider postponing unless you're determined and experienced.

Above 25 mph (40 km/h) Dangerous
Score: 5/100

Just Don't: Whole trees sway. Walking is difficult. This isn't grilling weather; it's "secure loose objects and stay inside" weather. Embers can travel far, grills can tip, and your dinner can become a safety incident.

Understanding Gusts

Weather reports typically show two wind numbers: sustained speed and gusts. Both matter, but in different ways.

Sustained Wind

The average wind speed over a period (usually 2 minutes). This tells you what to expect most of the time. It's your baseline for planning grill position and fuel needs.

Wind Gusts

Brief increases in wind speed, typically lasting only seconds. Gusts can be 50-100% higher than sustained wind. They're what knock things over and cause sudden flame flare-ups.

Our Approach to Gusts

We factor in gusts at 70% of their reported value when calculating your wind score. Why not 100%? Because gusts are brief and intermittent. A 30 mph gust during otherwise 10 mph winds is concerning but not as impactful as sustained 30 mph wind.

Formula: Effective Wind = max(Sustained Wind, Gust Speed × 0.70)

Wind Direction Strategy

Knowing where the wind comes from helps you position your grill for optimal performance.

1

Back to the Wind

Position your grill so its back faces the wind direction. The grill body becomes its own wind break, protecting the cooking surface and maintaining more consistent heat.

2

Never Side-On

Wind hitting your grill from the side creates maximum heat disruption. It pulls heat across and away from your food, creating severe hot/cold zones.

3

Position Yourself Upwind

Stand upwind of the grill so smoke blows away from you, not into your face. This seems obvious until you're crying into your burger because you forgot.

4

Check Direction Changes

Wind direction can shift throughout the day, especially near bodies of water or in hilly areas. Be prepared to reposition if conditions change significantly.

Creating Wind Breaks

When wind is unavoidable, create barriers to protect your grilling space.

Natural Barriers

Position your grill near (not against) walls, fences, or hedges. Keep at least 3-5 feet clearance for safety and ventilation. Buildings work well; wooden fences need more distance.

Portable Wind Screens

Folding camping wind screens or even large pieces of plywood can be positioned to block wind. Ensure they're weighted or staked so they don't become projectiles.

Vehicle Positioning

In a pinch, parking a car or truck to create a wind shadow works surprisingly well. Just maintain safe distance from the grill to avoid heat damage or fire risk.

Safety First

Never grill in an enclosed space or garage, even to escape wind. Carbon monoxide buildup is deadly, and the fire risk is extreme. Wind breaks should reduce wind, not eliminate ventilation.
